Autonomous mobile robots operating in GPS-denied unstructured environments---sandy
terrain, planetary analogues, agricultural fields, and industrial inspection
sites---require reliable localization that functions without visual
features, structured infrastructure, or expensive motion-capture rigs.
We present \textbf{DUNE} (Dual-tag UWB Navigation Engine), an ultra-wideband
real-time localization system delivering full six-degree-of-freedom
(6-DOF: $x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi$) pose estimation for a 13-DOF
rocker--bogie mobile manipulator.
Three contributions distinguish DUNE from all prior Decawave DW1000 implementations:
(i)~a dual-tag geometry providing metric heading directly from two tag positions,
combined with a five-anchor 3D deployment and BNO085 IMU fusion for complete
roll--pitch--yaw and XYZ;
(ii)~a two-tier in-situ calibration---per-board antenna-delay binary search
followed by a position-dependent spatial error map---validated against a documented
double-correction failure in naive software-bias approaches;
and (iii)~same-frame first-path/receive-power NLOS scoring with continuous weighted
multilateration, correcting a cross-link measurement error present in all
existing libraries.
Experiments on a sandy indoor testbed with an overhead Kinect~v2 + AprilTag
ground-truth system demonstrate \todo{X}\,cm 2D RMSE at \SI{\approx10}{\hertz}.
